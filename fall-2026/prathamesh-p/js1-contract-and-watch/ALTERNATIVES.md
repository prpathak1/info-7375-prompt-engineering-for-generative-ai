# Alternatives: what an existing tool already does

## (a) Documented behavior (not hands-on), retrieved 2026-10-31 by Claude in claude.ai from LinkedIn Help and third-party guides

- LinkedIn job alerts are created from a job search, using an alert toggle. They deliver new postings that match the saved search on a chosen schedule
  (daily, weekly or real-time).
- A member can hold up to 20 alerts.
- An optional recommendations setting adds jobs based on alerts, activity and profile.
- The sources consulted do not describe an alert explaining why a posting matched, citing posting text, or flagging sponsorship or clearance wording.

What I saw: LinkedIn job alerts (Prathamesh, 2026-10-31, ~14:00)
I looked at LinkedIn job search results (the same search a job alert is built from), not a saved alert's email.
I searched "network engineer" in Boston and opened 3 results. 2 loaded; the LEVELOCITI posting didn't load.

* Why it matched: Neither posting gave a reason up front. Both only had "Use AI to assess how you fit" with a "Show match details" button, so you have to click to see any reasoning. The only "you" signal on the page was in the applicant stats, where "Master's Degree" was marked "Similar to you."
* Sponsorship or visa info: Nothing in either header. Parallel Wireless's requirements say candidates must "be authorized to work in the U.S. without sponsorship," but LinkedIn didn't flag it, and you only find it by reading the description. Wizards of the Coast says nothing about sponsorship anywhere in the posting.
* Seen before / posting date: Both say "Reposted" (2 months ago and 3 months ago) and both say "No longer accepting applications," yet both still appeared in search. Neither showed a "Viewed" tag. The applicant counts don't match: the Parallel Wireless header says "Over 100 applicants," while the panel lower down says 525.
* Other mismatches I noticed: The "Network Engineer" title at Wizards asks for 6+ years, but 66% of people who clicked apply were entry level. The Parallel Wireless card says "Greater Boston," but the description says the job is in Nashua, NH, 45 miles north.

Difference from my agent: LinkedIn showed me closed postings, hid the match reasoning behind a click, and left the sponsorship requirement and seniority level buried in the description. My agent should check the posting is still open, quote the sponsorship line (or label it "posting is silent"), and flag experience requirements before calling anything relevant.
